02 · Approach

Align first, colorize second.

Each plate stores three monochrome exposures stacked vertically in blue, green, red order. I split the plate, keep blue fixed, and translate green and red until their similarity to blue is maximal. The shifted channels are then stacked into the final RGB image.

NCC(A, B) = Σ(A − Ā)(B − B̄) / √[Σ(A − Ā)² Σ(B − B̄)²]

NCC is computed on exposure values over a shared overlapping interior. Higher scores indicate a better match.

Why a pyramid?

Searching a large displacement range directly at full resolution is expensive. Instead, I estimate a rough displacement at the smallest level and propagate it upward, refining only a small neighborhood at every finer resolution.

pixels used for NCC
10% border ignored

Why ignore the border?

The scanned plates contain strong black and white borders. These borders can dominate NCC and produce a false optimum. I therefore ignore the outer 10% of the image during pyramid scoring. This does not crop the final image; it only changes which pixels contribute to the matching score.

Offset convention: (x, y) is the displacement applied to the named channel. Positive x moves right; positive y moves down.

06 · Failure analysis

Where raw-pixel NCC breaks.

Emir of Bukhara

Green aligns at (24, 49), but the baseline red channel lands at (-205, 141). The red and blue exposures have substantially different brightness patterns, so raw-intensity NCC finds a false correlation rather than the correct geometric correspondence.

What I changed

Instead of changing the pyramid or search strategy, I changed only the feature representation used during scoring. The optional experiment below computes Sobel gradient magnitude before NCC so that the search compares structural boundaries rather than absolute brightness.

07 · Bells & Whistles

Better features: Sobel-gradient alignment.

The assignment suggests using gradients or edges when RGB intensity similarity is unreliable. I tested that idea on the one supplied image where the baseline clearly fails: Emir of Bukhara.

In the baseline method, each candidate translation is scored using NCC on raw grayscale intensity. This assumes that corresponding objects have similar brightness across the blue, green, and red exposures.

For strongly colored materials this assumption can break: a region can be bright through one filter and dark through another even though its geometric location is unchanged.

Why gradients help

Sobel gradient magnitude emphasizes local intensity changes instead of absolute brightness. Boundaries such as clothing outlines, windows, and architectural edges tend to occur at the same geometric locations across all three exposures even when their brightness differs.

The image pyramid, translation-only motion model, NCC objective, coarse and refinement radii, and 10% scoring crop are unchanged. Only the representation used to score each candidate translation changes.

Gradients are used only to estimate the displacement. Once the best displacement is found, I apply that translation to the original grayscale red and green exposures and stack those original channels into the final RGB image.

08 · Takeaways

What worked.

Raw-intensity NCC plus a coarse-to-fine pyramid is sufficient for nearly the entire supplied set. Border-aware scoring improves stability, and gradient features provide a targeted fix for a case where intensity correspondence is unreliable.

Single scale Simple exhaustive search is enough for the low-resolution JPEGs.
Image pyramid Large TIFF displacements become practical with coarse-to-fine refinement.
Border-aware NCC Ignoring unreliable plate borders prevents false correlations.
Gradient features Sobel edges handle cross-channel brightness differences more robustly.
