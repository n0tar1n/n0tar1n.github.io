Method

One alignment pipeline

Each input contains three monochrome exposures stacked vertically in blue, green, red order. I split the image, hold blue fixed, and translate green and red until their NCC score against blue is maximal. The aligned channels are then stacked into an RGB image.

  • Small JPEGs: exhaustive search in a ±15 px window.
  • Large TIFFs: a 0.5-scale image pyramid; search ±15 px at the coarsest level, then ±2 px around the scaled estimate at each level.
  • Border handling: score only the shared interior and ignore the outer 10% to reduce plate-border influence.

Offset convention: (x, y) is the translation applied to the named channel; positive x moves right and positive y moves down.

Failure and extras

What did not work

Emir of Bukhara is the one clear failure. Green aligns at (24, 49), but raw-pixel NCC selects an incorrect red displacement of (-205, 141). Its red and blue exposures have substantially different brightness patterns, so intensity correlation chooses a false match. Aligning edges or gradients would be a sensible improvement, but this page reports the required raw-pixel NCC result.

Bells and whistles: border cropping during scoring and a coarse-to-fine pyramid. Together, they keep the method simple while making large-displacement TIFF alignment practical and more stable.
