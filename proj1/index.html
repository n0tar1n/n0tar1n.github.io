Overview

From three monochrome exposures to one RGB photograph

Each digitized plate contains three grayscale exposures stacked vertically in blue, green, red order. I split the plate into three equal images, keep blue fixed, and estimate translations that align green and red to blue. The final RGB image is formed by stacking the aligned channels.

Offset convention on this page: all displacements are reported as (x, y), where positive x moves a channel right and positive y moves it down.

Approach

Alignment metric and coarse-to-fine search

01

Split and normalize

The input plate is converted to floating point values in [0,1] and split into equally sized B, G, and R exposures.

02

Score with NCC

I use mean-subtracted normalized cross-correlation. Higher NCC indicates better agreement between the translated channel and the blue reference.

NCC(a,b) = ((a−ā) · (b−b̄)) / (‖a−ā‖ ‖b−b̄‖)

03

Ignore unreliable borders

Candidate shifts are scored only on a shared interior region. For pyramid alignment, the outer 10% is also ignored during scoring so dark glass-plate borders do not dominate NCC.

04

Coarse-to-fine pyramid

For large TIFFs, images are repeatedly resized by 0.5. I search ±15 pixels at the coarsest level, scale that estimate upward, then search only ±2 pixels around the prediction at each finer level.

Single-scale window ±15 px Pyramid scale 0.5 Coarse radius 15 px Refine radius 2 px Min. pyramid size 200 px Scoring crop 10%

Failure analysis

Emir of Bukhara: where raw-pixel NCC breaks down

The green channel aligns well at (24, 49), but NCC chooses a clearly incorrect red displacement of (−205, 141). The red and blue exposures of the Emir have very different brightness patterns, so matching raw intensity values can prefer a false correlation even when geometric content is not aligned.

This is the single clear failure in the 17-image set. A natural improvement would be to align edge or gradient features rather than raw intensities, but I keep the baseline NCC result here because the project specifically asks for NCC/L2-based results.

Takeaways

What worked

A simple translation model plus NCC works surprisingly well for most of the collection. The key engineering improvement is the image pyramid: it expands the effective displacement range while keeping full-resolution computation small. Cropping the scoring region away from plate borders also made the matching much more stable, especially for images such as Church.

The final pipeline uses one fixed parameter set and processes full-resolution TIFFs in roughly three seconds each, while preserving the straightforward NCC baseline required by the assignment.
