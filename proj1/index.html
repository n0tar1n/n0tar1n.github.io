01 · Approach

Align first, colorize second.

Each plate stores three monochrome exposures stacked vertically in blue, green, red order. I split the plate, keep blue fixed, and translate green and red until their similarity to blue is maximal. The shifted channels are then stacked into the final RGB image.

NCC(A, B)=Σ(A − Ā)(B − B̄) / √[Σ(A − Ā)² Σ(B − B̄)²]

NCC is computed on raw exposure pixel values over the overlapping interior. Higher scores indicate a better match.

05 · Failure analysis & extras

Where raw-pixel NCC breaks.

Emir of Bukhara

Green aligns at (24, 49), but red lands at an implausible (-205, 141). The red and blue exposures have substantially different brightness patterns, so raw-pixel NCC finds a false correlation rather than geometric alignment. Edge or gradient alignment would be a useful next experiment.

Bells & whistles

Two implementation details improve the baseline: I exclude the outer 10% from scoring to avoid plate borders, and use a coarse-to-fine pyramid to make large-displacement TIFF alignment practical. The final channel shifts still use the required NCC pipeline.
